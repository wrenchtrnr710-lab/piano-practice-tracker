# 🎹 Piano Practice Tracker

A beautiful, interactive practice companion for your daily piano routine!

## What's Included

This tracker helps you stay organized with your 28-minute daily practice routine:

### 1. **A Dozen a Day (Book 1)** - 8 minutes
- Select from all 50 exercises across 5 groups
- Timer to keep you on track
- Checkbox to mark when you've completed 4 repetitions

### 2. **Scales & Chord Progressions** - 10 minutes
- 8 Major scales (C, G, D, A, E, B, F, B♭)
- 8 Relative minor scales
- Learn Roman numeral notation (I, IV, V for major; i, iv, v for minor)
- Diminished chords marked with ° for learning
- Track left hand, right hand, and both hands practice
- Built-in metronome link at 60 BPM

### 3. **Kindle Book: Piano Exercises for Beginners** - 10 minutes
- Direct link to your Kindle book
- Checkbox to mark completion

## Features

- ⏱️ **Individual Timers** for each section
- 🎵 **Metronome Link** for keeping tempo
- 📊 **Progress Log** to track your practice history
- 💾 **Auto-Save** - your daily progress is saved automatically
- 📱 **Responsive Design** - works on any screen size
- 🎨 **Beautiful UI** - clean, modern interface optimized for practice

## How to Use

1. Open the website in your browser
2. Select your exercise for "A Dozen a Day"
3. Click "Start Timer" to begin
4. Select your scale for the day
5. Use the metronome link if needed
6. Check off each completed section
7. Track your progress in the log below

## Scales & Roman Numeral Notation

The tracker displays chord progressions using Roman numerals:
- **Uppercase (I, IV, V, VI)** = Major chords
- **Lowercase (i, iv, v)** = Minor chords
- **With ° symbol (vii°)** = Diminished chords

This is the standard notation used globally in music theory!

## Data Storage

Your progress is saved locally in your browser. This means:
- Your practice history stays on this computer
- No data is sent to any server
- Clearing your browser cache will delete the logs
- Each day starts fresh!

## Tips

- Set a consistent practice time each day
- Use the metronome at a comfortable tempo
- Don't rush through the exercises
- Practice scales slowly to focus on fingering
- Log your progress daily to stay motivated
